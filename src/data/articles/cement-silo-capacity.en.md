# Cement Silo Capacity & Selection Guide: How to Size Storage for Your Concrete Batching Plant

A correctly specified cement silo does more than store powdered material. It protects production continuity, supports consistent material supply, reduces logistical pressure and helps concrete manufacturers manage their operating costs.

An undersized silo can expose an entire batching plant to delivery delays. An oversized silo can increase capital expenditure, foundation requirements and installation costs without delivering a meaningful operational benefit.

The correct storage capacity depends on production demand, cement consumption, bulk density, delivery frequency, available space and the reliability of the supply chain.

This guide explains how to calculate cement storage requirements, compare silo configurations, evaluate essential accessories and prepare a technically complete request for quotation.

## 01. What is a cement silo?

A cement silo is an industrial storage system designed to receive, contain and discharge cement or other compatible powdered materials.

Cement silos are commonly integrated into:

- Ready-mix concrete batching plants.
- Precast concrete manufacturing facilities.
- Cement distribution terminals.
- Dry mortar production plants.
- Infrastructure and construction projects.
- Industrial powder handling installations.

In a typical concrete batching plant, cement is delivered pneumatically from a bulk tanker, stored inside the silo and subsequently transferred through a mechanical feeding system to the weighing and mixing equipment.

The silo therefore acts as a buffer between the material supply chain and the production process.

Its effectiveness depends on three distinct capabilities:

| Capability | Operational purpose                                |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Receiving  | Accept material deliveries safely and efficiently  |
| Storage    | Maintain sufficient usable inventory               |
| Discharge  | Supply material at the rate required by production |

A silo with sufficient storage capacity but inadequate discharge performance can still become a production bottleneck.

## 02. Cement silo capacity: Understanding tonnes versus cubic metres

Cement silos are frequently advertised using nominal capacities such as 50 tonnes, 100 tonnes or 200 tonnes.

However, the physical storage volume is measured in cubic metres, while the actual stored mass depends on the bulk density of the material.

The fundamental relationship is:

**Material mass (t) = Material volume (m³) × Bulk density (t/m³)**

Bulk density represents the mass of powder occupying a given volume, including the spaces between particles.

It must not be confused with the solid particle density of cement.

The bulk density of a powder can change according to its composition, aeration, compaction, moisture condition and handling history.

### Why nominal capacity is not usable capacity

A silo cannot necessarily be filled to its complete geometric volume.

Space may be required above the material for ventilation, filling protection and approved operating limits. Some material may also remain unavailable for normal production because of the discharge characteristics of the equipment.

**[Figure 1 — Nominal volume versus usable inventory]**

For preliminary planning:

**Usable inventory = Nominal volume × Bulk density × Approved fill fraction − Unavailable material allowance**

The result must also remain within the silo's structural and approved operating limits.

### Example: An 80 m³ cement silo

Consider the following illustrative specifications:

| Parameter                        | Assumed value |
| -------------------------------- | ------------: |
| Nominal geometric volume         |         80 m³ |
| Cement bulk density              |     1.20 t/m³ |
| Assumed usable filling fraction  |           90% |
| Calculated maximum material mass |        86.4 t |
| Unavailable material allowance   |           4 t |
| Estimated usable inventory       |        82.4 t |

Although the theoretical mass at full geometric volume would be 96 tonnes, the assumed usable inventory is only 82.4 tonnes.

The 90% filling fraction is an illustrative calculation input, not a universal safe filling recommendation.

Always obtain the approved maximum material level, permissible stored mass and expected discharge characteristics from the equipment supplier.

## 03. Calculate your concrete plant's daily cement consumption

Before selecting a silo, establish the amount of cement consumed by the production process.

Concrete plant output is generally expressed in cubic metres per hour, while cement dosage is expressed in kilograms per cubic metre of concrete.

The daily consumption formula is:

**Daily cement consumption (t) = Daily concrete production (m³) × Cement dosage (kg/m³) ÷ 1,000**

### Example: A medium-sized concrete batching operation

Assume a batching plant produces:

- 100 m³ of concrete per operating day.
- An average cement dosage of 320 kg/m³.

Daily cement consumption:

100 × 320 ÷ 1,000 = **32 tonnes per day.**

A silo with 80 tonnes of usable material would provide approximately 2.5 operating days of production without replenishment.

### Cement consumption by production volume

| Daily concrete production | 250 kg/m³ dosage | 320 kg/m³ dosage | 400 kg/m³ dosage |
| ------------------------: | ---------------: | ---------------: | ---------------: |
|                     50 m³ |           12.5 t |             16 t |             20 t |
|                    100 m³ |             25 t |             32 t |             40 t |
|                    150 m³ |           37.5 t |             48 t |             60 t |
|                    200 m³ |             50 t |             64 t |             80 t |
|                    300 m³ |             75 t |             96 t |            120 t |
|                    500 m³ |            125 t |            160 t |            200 t |

These figures are mathematical examples. Actual cement dosage must follow the approved concrete mix design.

### Rated production versus actual production

A batching plant rated at 60 m³/h does not necessarily produce 480 m³ during every eight-hour shift.

Actual production may be affected by:

- Customer demand.
- Truck availability.
- Mixing cycle duration.
- Material replenishment.
- Equipment maintenance.
- Production scheduling.
- Changes in concrete mix designs.

Use representative production records whenever available. For new installations, prepare low-demand, normal-demand and peak-demand scenarios.

Storage should be evaluated against realistic peak consumption, not only average annual production.

## 04. Determine your minimum reserve and reorder point

Daily consumption alone is insufficient to specify storage capacity.

The material supply chain must also be considered.

Two plants consuming identical quantities of cement may require different silo capacities if one receives reliable local deliveries while the other depends on long-distance transportation.

### Delivery lead time

Delivery lead time is the period between placing an order and receiving the material.

It may include supplier preparation, vehicle availability, travel and delivery scheduling.

### Safety stock

Safety stock is additional inventory intended to absorb operational uncertainty, including delayed deliveries and higher-than-expected demand.

A simple preliminary estimate is:

**Safety stock = Daily cement consumption × Reserve duration in operating days**

For a plant consuming 32 tonnes per day, an assumed half-day reserve represents 16 tonnes.

### Reorder point

The reorder point indicates when replenishment should be initiated.

**Reorder point = Expected consumption during delivery lead time + Safety stock**

Using the same example:

| Parameter                |             Value |
| ------------------------ | ----------------: |
| Daily cement consumption |              32 t |
| Delivery lead time       |   1 operating day |
| Safety stock duration    | 0.5 operating day |
| Safety stock             |              16 t |
| Reorder point            |              48 t |

Under these assumptions, replenishment should be initiated when the available inventory reaches approximately 48 tonnes.

For high-throughput plants, this calculation should be integrated into an inventory-position system that also accounts for confirmed incoming deliveries. Multiple orders may need to be outstanding simultaneously.

## 05. Ensure your silo can receive a complete tanker delivery

One frequently overlooked aspect of silo procurement is the size of incoming cement deliveries.

Consider a plant purchasing cement in 28-tonne tanker loads.

If the silo has only 15 tonnes of verified remaining storage capacity, a complete tanker load cannot be accepted.

This can create delivery delays, partial-load complications and additional logistical costs.

For a conservative preliminary sizing scenario, assume that a full tanker load could arrive immediately when usable inventory reaches the reorder point.

**Target working inventory capacity = Reorder point + Full tanker delivery mass**

Using the previous example:

48 t + 28 t = **76 tonnes of usable inventory capacity.**

If the delivery schedule is reliable and consumption continues before the vehicle arrives, a more detailed inventory simulation may justify a different storage requirement.

### Calculate the required geometric volume

Assuming:

- Required usable inventory: 76 tonnes.
- Unavailable material allowance: 4 tonnes.
- Bulk density: 1.20 t/m³.
- Assumed filling fraction: 90%.

Required nominal volume:

(76 + 4) ÷ (1.20 × 0.90) = **74.1 m³.**

An 80 m³ configuration would therefore be a reasonable candidate for further engineering assessment.

This example demonstrates why storage selection should begin with consumption and replenishment requirements rather than an advertised tonnage.

Before any actual delivery, the available safe receiving capacity must be verified using the approved site procedures and instrumentation.

Both conditions should be considered during equipment specification.

## 08. Which type of cement silo should you choose?

Storage systems can be supplied in different construction and installation configurations.

### Welded cement silos

Welded or monolithic silos are manufactured as a largely assembled vessel structure.

They can reduce the amount of vessel assembly required at the installation site.

Advantages include:

- Reduced on-site vessel assembly.
- Potentially shorter installation duration.
- Fewer field-assembled vessel joints.

Their main limitation is transportation. Fully assembled silos may require special transport arrangements depending on their dimensions and destination.

### Bolted cement silos

Bolted silos are manufactured in sections that are transported separately and assembled at the project site.

This configuration can improve transport efficiency, especially for larger structures and international deliveries.

Important considerations include:

- Assembly labour and lifting equipment.
- Manufacturer-approved bolting and sealing procedures.
- Quality control during installation.
- Joint inspection and long-term maintenance.

### Horizontal cement silos

Horizontal silos use a lower-profile storage arrangement and can be appropriate for temporary projects, restricted-height installations and certain mobile concrete production operations.

Their discharge arrangements may differ substantially from conventional vertical gravity-fed silos.

### Telescopic cement silos

Telescopic configurations reduce transport dimensions by allowing parts of the silo structure to be assembled or extended at the destination.

They can be useful where transport geometry is a major project constraint.

### Configuration comparison

| Factor                     | Welded vertical         | Bolted vertical           | Horizontal            | Telescopic                 |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Site assembly              | Generally lower         | Higher                    | Model dependent       | Required                   |
| Transport constraints      | Potentially significant | Modular transport         | Model dependent       | Reduced transport envelope |
| Installed height           | Higher                  | Higher                    | Lower                 | Higher when assembled      |
| Ground footprint           | Often compact           | Often compact             | Generally greater     | Often compact              |
| Relocation suitability     | Project dependent       | More complex              | Potentially favorable | Project dependent          |
| Typical selection priority | Installation simplicity | Transport and scalability | Height and mobility   | Transport dimensions       |

The correct configuration should be selected according to total installed cost, project duration, available space, transport limitations and structural requirements.

## 09. Is one large silo better than multiple smaller silos?

A plant may achieve the required total storage capacity using one large silo or several smaller units.

For example, consider a preliminary comparison between one 200-tonne nominal silo and two 100-tonne nominal silos.

### One large silo

Potential benefits:

- Fewer independent storage vessels.
- Potentially simpler accessory arrangements.
- Reduced duplication of certain equipment.

Potential limitations:

- A single storage-system failure may interrupt the entire material supply.
- Different cement types cannot be stored separately in an unpartitioned vessel.
- Larger lifting, foundation or transport requirements may apply.

### Multiple smaller silos

Potential benefits:

- Separate storage of cement grades and mineral additions.
- Greater material selection flexibility.
- Potential operational redundancy when compatible systems are available.
- Possibility of phased capacity expansion.

Potential limitations:

- Additional filters, sensors, discharge systems and controls.
- Increased installation complexity.
- Potentially higher maintenance requirements.

Redundancy is only achieved when the conveying and control systems allow production to continue safely using the alternative storage vessel.

If two silos contain different materials, their combined capacity cannot be treated as interchangeable cement inventory.

## 10. Essential cement silo accessories and their functions

A silo should be evaluated as a complete material storage and handling system, not simply a steel vessel.

| Component                    | Function                                                 | What to verify                                 |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Filling pipe                 | Receives pneumatic material deliveries                   | Diameter, connection and compatibility         |
| Vent filter                  | Allows conveying air to leave while controlling dust     | Airflow, filtration and maintenance access     |
| Pressure protection          | Protects against unsafe pressure conditions              | Design basis and applicable requirements       |
| High-level detector          | Detects material approaching the permitted filling limit | Sensor location and protection integration     |
| Continuous level measurement | Supports inventory monitoring                            | Accuracy under actual powder conditions        |
| Load cells                   | Estimate stored material mass through weighing           | Capacity, accuracy and installation design     |
| Isolation valve              | Controls or isolates material discharge                  | Material compatibility and safe service access |
| Fluidisation equipment       | Supports powder flow where required                      | Material-specific design                       |
| Screw conveyor               | Transfers cement to downstream equipment                 | Required throughput and routing                |
| Access ladder and platform   | Provides maintenance access                              | Fall protection and safe access design         |
| Control panel                | Integrates monitoring and protection functions           | Alarms, interlocks and plant compatibility     |

### Level measurement is not always weight measurement

A high-level detector provides a warning at a defined material level. It does not necessarily provide a continuous measurement of stored tonnage.

Continuous level instruments estimate the material level, while load-cell arrangements can provide mass-related measurements when properly engineered and calibrated.

Powder surface geometry, material density variation and aeration can affect inventory estimates.

For operations that depend heavily on accurate replenishment scheduling, evaluate the appropriate measurement technology before purchasing the silo.

## 11. Selecting the correct screw conveyor capacity

A storage silo must supply cement fast enough to support the batching plant's production requirements.

Consider a plant producing 60 m³ of concrete per hour with a cement dosage of 320 kg/m³.

The average cement demand at rated output is:

60 × 320 ÷ 1,000 = **19.2 tonnes per hour.**

However, a concrete batching plant does not necessarily consume cement continuously.

Assume that the batching system requires 640 kg of cement for a particular batch, and the permitted cement feeding period is 45 seconds.

The equivalent instantaneous average feeding requirement is:

0.64 ÷ (45 ÷ 3,600) = **51.2 tonnes per hour.**

This demonstrates why screw conveyor selection cannot be based solely on average hourly cement consumption.

Actual conveyor specification must account for:

- Required batch mass and feeding window.
- Conveyor length and inclination.
- Powder characteristics.
- Inlet and outlet arrangement.
- Starting and operating torque.
- Motor and gearbox selection.
- Feeding accuracy and control strategy.
- Actual installed conveying capacity.

Request manufacturer performance data for the proposed installation geometry.

## 12. Silo safety: Ventilation, pressure protection and overfilling

Pneumatic tanker deliveries introduce both powdered material and conveying air into the silo.

If the air cannot escape through an appropriately designed ventilation system, pressure can accumulate inside the structure.

A blocked vent filter, unsuitable filling conditions or a malfunctioning protection system can create a serious industrial safety hazard.

The receiving silo must not be treated as if it has the same pressure rating as the delivery tanker.

An appropriate silo protection system may include:

- Correctly engineered ventilation.
- Pressure monitoring and alarms.
- High-level protection.
- Pressure relief devices.
- Filling interruption systems.
- Clearly defined inspection and maintenance arrangements.

These systems must be evaluated together rather than treated as independent accessories.

Pressure relief devices are not intended to replace normal ventilation or safe operating controls.

Unusual pressure alarms, unexpected powder releases and repeated protection-system activations must be investigated by competent personnel under approved procedures.

Never bypass safety systems or attempt to compensate for poor unloading performance by increasing delivery pressure beyond approved limits.

Industry guidance from the Mineral Products Association and Resources Safety & Health Queensland provides additional information on the prevention of silo over-pressurisation.

## 13. Foundation, site preparation and installation requirements

The silo purchase price represents only part of the complete installation.

A vertical silo transfers the weight of its structure and stored material to its supporting system and foundation.

The foundation design must also account for relevant project-specific environmental and operational loads.

Important engineering considerations include:

- Maximum approved stored mass.
- Silo and accessory dead loads.
- Wind exposure.
- Seismic requirements where applicable.
- Soil bearing conditions and settlement.
- Support reactions and anchorage.
- Maintenance and access loads.
- Installation and lifting conditions.

Before preparing the foundation, obtain the approved general arrangement drawing and structural load information from the equipment supplier.

Foundation dimensions and anchor arrangements must be designed by qualified professionals for the actual installation site.

### Site planning considerations

| Site requirement         | Procurement question                                           |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ground footprint         | Is adequate installation and access space available?           |
| Height restriction       | Does the complete silo fit within site limitations?            |
| Tanker access            | Can delivery vehicles reach the filling point safely?          |
| Crane access             | Can the selected structure be erected and serviced?            |
| Maintenance space        | Are filters, valves and instruments accessible?                |
| Electrical supply        | Are suitable voltage, power and control connections available? |
| Batching plant interface | Can the discharge equipment reach the receiving hopper?        |
| Future expansion         | Is space available for additional storage units?               |

Resolving these questions before ordering equipment can reduce expensive on-site modifications.

## 14. Calculate the financial impact of insufficient storage

The commercial value of a cement silo is closely related to its ability to support uninterrupted production.

Consider a ready-mix plant operating under the following illustrative conditions:

| Parameter                            | Assumed value |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------: |
| Production during operating periods  |       40 m³/h |
| Contribution margin per cubic metre  |           €12 |
| Cement-related downtime per month    |       4 hours |
| Operating months per year            |            12 |
| Annual interrupted production time   |      48 hours |
| Potential annual production affected |      1,920 m³ |

If this production cannot be recovered later, the illustrative annual contribution margin at risk is:

1,920 × €12 = **€23,040.**

Suppose an expanded storage installation costs an additional €25,000 and is expected to prevent 70% of these losses.

The estimated annual contribution recovered would be €16,128.

The corresponding illustrative simple payback is approximately 18.6 months.

This calculation excludes financing, additional maintenance, inventory carrying costs, taxes and other project-specific expenses.

It also assumes sufficient customer demand and that the interrupted production would otherwise be permanently lost.

Additional storage is not automatically profitable. Its benefits must be evaluated against the actual causes of production interruption.

There is no meaningful universal cement silo price without defining the equipment and installation scope.

The principal cost factors include:

## 17. Frequently asked questions

### What size cement silo do I need?

Calculate daily cement consumption, required reserve inventory, delivery lead time and tanker delivery quantity. Convert the resulting inventory requirement into nominal volume using verified bulk density and approved operating limits.

### How many cubic metres does a 100-tonne cement silo have?

At an assumed density of 1.20 t/m³, 100 tonnes of cement occupies approximately 83.3 m³ of material volume. The required geometric volume may be greater because of filling restrictions and operating allowances.

### How long does cement last in a silo?

Storage suitability depends on moisture protection, material specifications, inventory turnover and the cement manufacturer's recommendations. There is no universal storage duration applicable to every cement product and installation.

### Can fly ash and cement be stored in the same silo?

A suitable silo may be used for different materials at different times, provided compatibility, contamination control, structural ratings and discharge performance are verified. Separate storage is generally required when different materials must remain independently available.

### Is a bolted silo better than a welded silo?

Bolted construction can improve transport efficiency and enable larger modular configurations. Welded construction can reduce vessel assembly at the destination. The better choice depends on logistics, site conditions and total installed cost.

### Can a horizontal cement silo be used for a stationary batching plant?

Potentially. Horizontal silos may be appropriate when height restrictions or particular installation requirements favor a lower-profile arrangement.

### Can an existing silo be expanded?

Additional storage can sometimes be integrated into an existing plant. Modification of an existing silo structure requires appropriate engineering assessment and approval.

### Does every cement silo include a screw conveyor?

No. The discharge and conveying equipment may be quoted separately. Verify whether the proposed configuration includes the conveyor, motor, gearbox, mounting arrangements and controls.

### Is a silo pressure relief valve sufficient for safe pneumatic filling?

No. Safe filling depends on the complete ventilation, monitoring, protection and operating system.
